HEADLINE: S Korea Sept petrochemical export value up 5%; volumes down 19%

PUBLISHED: 01-Oct-2026

ATTRIBUTION: Jonathan Yee (ICIS)

BODY:

SINGAPORE (ICIS)–South Korea's petrochemical exports in September rose by 5.1% year on year to $3.9 billion on higher prices but volumes continued to fall amid low production.

Overall full-year 2026 exports are projected to reach a record $1 trillion on the strength of semiconductor shipments.

- Average Sept petrochemical export prices rise 30.0% on year
- Overall Sept trade surplus at nearly $50 billion on strong AI-related demand
- Sept manufacturing PMI rises to 53.9 from 52.3 in Aug

By volume, petrochemical exports in September fell by 19.1% year on year to 2.8 million tonnes, according to data from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources (MOTIR) on Thursday.

The average export prices of petrochemicals rose by 30.0% year on year to $1,416/tonne in September as feedstock prices, including crude oil and naphtha, spiked amid the Middle East conflict.

Meanwhile, imports of chemical products on 1-25 September were up by 17.0% year on year, the ministry said.

OVERALL EXPORTS AT ALL-TIME HIGHS

South Korea's total exports in September surged by 83.5% year on year to an all-time high of $120.9 billion, while imports increased by 26.0% to $71.1 billion, resulting in a record trade surplus of around $49.9 billion.

This is the first time South Korea's trade surplus has exceeded $40 billion.

Semiconductors remained the key export driver, with exports more than tripling year on year to a record $60.3 billion, exceeding $60 billion for the first time, the MOTIR said.

September exports excluding semiconductors grew by 23% year on year.

Among product segments, automobiles posted a 5.5% year-on-year decline in exports to $6.1 billion, weighed down by fewer working days following the shift of the three-day Chuseok holiday to late September this year (24-26 September) from October in the previous year, as well as weak shipments of used cars.

South Korea's exports increased to six out of the country's nine major trading partners in September, namely, China, the US, ASEAN, the EU, Japan and India.

However, exports to the Middle East fell by 14.1% to $1.6 billion as difficulties in navigating the Strait of Hormuz reduced exports of automobiles, automobile components and general machinery.

September energy imports rose by 38.0% year on year to $13.0 billion as a 2.0% increase in crude import volumes and a 35.7% rise in import prices lifted South Korea's crude import expenditure by 38.4%.

The cumulative trade surplus for January-September 2026 stood at $252.5 billion, up from $49.8 billion in the same period last year and setting a new record.

For the whole of 2026, total exports could reach $1 trillion, but growing global protectionism and tensions in the Middle East remained significant variables, South Korea's industry minister Kim Jung-kwan said.

MANUFACTURING ACTIVITY STRENGTHENS

Separately, South Korea's manufacturing activity expanded at a faster pace in September, with the seasonally adjusted S&P Global purchasing managers' index (PMI) rising to 53.9 from 52.3 in August.

A reading above 50 indicates growth in manufacturing output, while a reading below 50 indicates contraction.

Production and new orders recorded their strongest growth since early 2021, supported particularly by demand from the semiconductor and automotive sectors, S&P Global said on Thursday.

New orders rose at the fastest pace since February 2021, while production growth was the strongest in five-and-a-half years, S&P Global economist Usamah Bhatti said.

Manufacturers increased purchasing activity and employment to meet higher output requirements, while backlogs of work rose at the fastest rate since May 2022.

Input cost inflation eased to its lowest level since October 2025, although manufacturers continued to report higher fuel and electrical-component prices. Selling-price inflation also moderated to the weakest level in the current 10-month period of rising charges, according to Bhatti.

Business confidence improved to its highest level since January, supported by expectations of continued growth in the semiconductor sector and a further normalization in price pressures, the S&P global economist said.
