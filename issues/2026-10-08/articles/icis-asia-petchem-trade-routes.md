HEADLINE: INSIGHT: Asia petrochemical trade routes shift on arbitrage opportunities, supply gaps
PUBLISHED: 07-Oct-2026
ATTRIBUTION: Jonathan Yee

BODY:
SINGAPORE (ICIS)–The US-Iran conflict has reshaped Asian petrochemical trade flows, prompting major importers to seek more cargoes from alternative sources as disrupted Middle East supply tightens availability and raises freight costs, though some routes may prove temporary as regional prices and arbitrage economics change.

- NE Asia suppliers expand aromatics shipments to south Asia, Turkey, Europe
- Alternative naphtha cargoes fail to fully replace disrupted Mideast supply
- Indian buyers explore Chinese PP, North American PE amid shipment delays

The clearest shifts have emerged in aromatics, where China, South Korea and Taiwan have expanded their roles as suppliers to other regions including India, Turkey and Europe.

NE ASIA AROMATICS EXPORTS WIDEN

China and South Korea have emerged as increasingly important sources of styrene monomer (SM) for India amid Middle East supply constraints.

In the first half of 2025, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait supplied about two-thirds of India's SM imports, but their combined share fell to roughly 26% in the first half of 2026, while China became the dominant origin with a share of around 42%, according to ICIS data.

South Korea has also emerged as an important additional supplier in the third quarter of 2026, after supplying around 17% to India in the first half of 2026.

Paraxylene (PX) trade flows have also shifted as Middle East supply tightened. PX from the Middle East is typically supplied to North America, south Asia and the Turkish market.

However, northeast Asian suppliers have emerged as alternative sources, as Taiwan exported around 30,000 tonnes of PX to India and 10,000 tonnes to Pakistan in July, redirecting cargoes that would otherwise largely have gone to China before the Middle East war.

Meanwhile, South Korea exported around 23,763 tonnes to Turkey in August while also increasing shipments to south Asia.

India's demand for imports is also set to grow, with GAIL targeting to start up its new downstream 1.25 million tonne/year purified terephthalic acid (PTA) unit within the current fiscal year ending March 2027.

In toluene, India has increased its reliance on South Korean supply amid constrained regional availability and snug domestic inventories.

South Korean exports to India rose from around 12,180 tonnes during 1-20 July to about 38,368 tonnes during 1-20 August, with total August loadings estimated at 54,130 tonnes.

Arbitrage opportunities have also moved Chinese exports to the US for the first time in June-July this year.

China exported 29,604 tonnes of toluene directly to the US for the first time in June-July 2026, while an additional 10,337 tonnes of Chinese mixed xylenes (MX) also flowed into the US market during the same period, according to ICIS data.

While South Korea has traditionally supplied toluene to the US, favourable export economics encouraged increased shipments from China during this period.

However, this new trade flow is expected to be temporary as US demand for high-octane aromatics blending components such as toluene and mixed xylenes is expected to weaken in the fourth quarter as the summer gasoline season ends.

Benzene exports from South Korea to the US also more than doubled in the first half of 2026 compared with 2025, while around 100,000 tonnes of benzene have shipped from India to Europe so in 2026, versus effectively zero in 2024 and 2025, making India a new significant benzene supplier to Europe.

In August, however, South Korean benzene exports shifted entirely to China amid low inventories, away from the US.

East China benzene inventories stood at 35,000 tonnes on 8 September, against 322,000 tonnes on 3 March.

China again accounted for all South Korea benzene exports during the first 20 days of September, based on market estimates.

NAPHTHA SUPPLY GAP PERSISTS

Asia entered the conflict heavily dependent on Middle Eastern naphtha, with volumes shipped through the Strait of Hormuz accounting for around 64% of the region's imports in 2025, according to ICIS data.

The United Arab Emirates (UAE), Qatar and Kuwait accounted for more than 75% of Middle Eastern naphtha shipments to Asia in January-February 2026, ICIS data showed.

Disruption through the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on refineries reduced Middle East export capacity, forcing some Asian crackers to trim operating rates or shut amid feedstock shortages.

While Saudi Arabia redirected some naphtha through the Red Sea, those shipments represent only a small proportion of the Middle East's previous exports of around 4 million tonnes/month to Asia, per ICIS data.

Sellers of Middle East naphtha have turned to export alternatives, such as ship-to-ship transfers that use routes avoiding the Strait of Hormuz.

As a result, Asian buyers have increased arbitrage imports from Europe, the Mediterranean, the US and Africa, while Russian naphtha has also played a larger role.

Russian naphtha is now priced broadly in line with other spot cargoes, according to market sources, after previously trading at a discount because of sanctions imposed following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

Given the urgent supply need, buyers in Japan and Taiwan paid spot premiums of around $100/tonne to Japan naphtha quotes for replacement cargoes, compared with less than $20/tonne before the conflict.

India is now the only country in Asia able to offer spot naphtha on a regular basis as it has some excess production.

INDIA DIVERSIFIES PE, PP IMPORT SOURCES

India is particularly reliant on imported low density polyethylene (LDPE) and certain polypropylene (PP) grades, which previously came largely from the Middle East.

Disruptions at Red Sea and Gulf ports, combined with higher crude and naphtha costs, pushed Asian polyethylene (PE) and PP prices to multi-month highs, although downstream demand remained subdued.

Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) producers faced increasing difficulty reaching traditional markets, leading to some redirecting cargoes to closer destinations in the eastern Mediterranean and Africa – where logistics risks are lower – or towards the spot market.

Meanwhile, Indian buyers faced delayed shipments, higher freight costs and lead times of up to around two months for some cargoes, prompting greater interest in Chinese PP and North American PE.

China is expecting about 4 million tonnes/year of PP capacity to come online in 2026, increasing the availability of material for export as domestic demand remains subdued.
