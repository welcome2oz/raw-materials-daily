HEADLINE: US Gulf producers shut in 9% of oil, 3% of natgas production amid tropical disturbance
PUBLISHED: 06-Oct-2026
ATTRIBUTION: Adam Yanelli

BODY:
HOUSTON (ICIS)–The US National Hurricane Center (NHC) is tracking a disturbance in the southern US Gulf with a high likelihood of becoming a named storm, but it is still too early to determine possible impacts to the northern Gulf coast.

But the storm has already led US Gulf producers to shut in 9.24% of oil and 3.36% of natural gas production, according to estimates from the Marine Minerals Administration (MMA).

The MMA was established in July to reunify the Bureau of Ocean Energy Management (BOEM) and the Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement (BSEE).

MMA said that no personnel have been evacuated from the 371 manned platforms in the US Gulf as of mid-afternoon on Tuesday.

No personnel have been evacuated from the 10 non-dynamically positioned rigs of this type currently operating in the Gulf, MMA said, and none of the 17 dynamically positioned rigs have moved off location.

The NHC said interests between the Florida panhandle west to Louisiana "should closely monitor the progress of this system" as it is expected to turn to the north or northeast toward the northern Gulf coast by the end of the week.

The NHC said advisories will likely be issued later today or tonight while the system moves slowly to the east.

Tropical storms, as well as those that strengthen into hurricanes, can prompt plant and refinery shutdowns or reduced operations as precaution measures ahead of the storms.

Severe weather conditions can cause plant or asset damage, resulting in unexpected or extended downtime.

Power outages can last for days or weeks.

Hurricanes shut down ports, railroads and highways, which can prevent operating plants from receiving feedstock or shipping out product.

Most US petrochemical plants and refineries are on the US Gulf Coast states of Texas and Louisiana, making them prone to hurricanes.
