HEADLINE: INSIGHT: Brazil court ruling threatens Braskem Idesa's bankruptcy exit
PUBLISHED: 06-Oct-2026
ATTRIBUTION: Al Greenwood

BODY:
HOUSTON (ICIS)–A court injunction in Brazil could prevent Braskem Idesa from securing all of the $486 million its majority shareholder would provide for its emergence from bankruptcy protection.

The injunction could be lifted if Braskem overturns it or addresses the court's concerns.

Otherwise, Braskem Idesa may need to find replacement funds. If that happens, Braskem's stake in the joint venture could be diluted.

Braskem disagrees with the ruling and intends to appeal it, the company said in court documents. Braskem expects the Brazilian court to make an expeditious decision.

Braskem did not comment beyond its public statements. Braskem Idesa did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

UP TO $486 MILLION AT STAKE

A key part of Braskem Idesa's reorganization plan calls for $486 million in funding from Braskem, according to the joint venture's disclosure statement. Those funds would be converted into equity if Braskem Idesa can consummate its reorganization plan and emerge from bankruptcy protection.

The plan was already confirmed by a US bankruptcy judge on September 24.

Despite achieving this milestone, the joint venture's pending exit from bankruptcy is being complicated by Braskem's separate restructuring proceedings in Brazil.

Braskem is pursuing an out-of-court restructuring, known recuperacao extrajudicial, in Brazil. The process is somewhat analogous to a prepackaged bankruptcy in the US.

On that same day that the US court confirmed Braskem Idesa's reorganization plan, a judge in the Brazilian court issued an injunction barring Braskem from releasing any more funds to Braskem Idesa.

BRAZILIAN COURT HALTS FURTHER FUNDING TO JV

In issuing the injunction, the court raised concerns that Braskem was assuming obligations without demonstrating that its Brazilian operations would receive something of equivalent value in return.

The court spelled out the concern in the ruling. "The imminent final approval of the transaction within US jurisdiction will result in the crystallization of financial commitments and the conversion of amounts into illiquid and subordinated equity interests, causing an irreversible drain on liquidity," according to an English translation of the Brazilian ruling.

This would complicate any attempts to recover the funds if the transaction were later found to be detrimental to creditors, according to the ruling.

As such, the court issued an emergency injunction barring Braskem from making further payments, transfers and other commitments called for by the reorganization plan. The order puts further funding under the $486 million package at risk.

The court also identified more than $300 million in potential obligations connected to future put obligations.

The order indicated that the court could reconsider the restrictions if Braskem provides additional information, including balance sheets, studies and contracts supporting the proposed transactions.

ORDER THREATENS BRASKEM IDESA'S BANKRUPTCY EXIT

Braskem could successfully overturn the injunction or satisfy the court's concern, which would allow it to disburse the remaining funds and for Braskem Idesa to emerge from bankruptcy protection.

Otherwise, Braskem Idesa could be forced to seek alternative financing or amend its plan, potentially affecting Braskem's stake in the reorganized company.

BRASKEM MAY LOSE MAJORITY STAKE

According to the disclosure statement, Braskem's $486 million contribution would be converted into a one-third stake in the reorganized Braskem Idesa.

This one-third stake would come on top of the 25% stake that Braskem would derive from its legacy ownership of Braskem Idesa.

Altogether, Braskem would hold a 58 1/3% stake in the reorganized Braskem Idesa. Holders of senior secured notes would see their holdings converted into a 33 1/3% stake in the reorganized company. The remaining 8 1/3% stake would go to Grupo Idesa, the other legacy joint venture partner.

If the court ultimately bars Braskem from disbursing the funds, then its 1/3 additional stake could be at risk.

The magnitude of the risk may depend on how much of the $486 million Braskem Idesa would need to replace.

The funds are divided into the following, according to the disclosure statement:

- The roll-up of $131 million in prepetition financing.
- $283 million in debtor-in-possession (DIP) financing.
- $71 million in equity contributions to be made on Braskem Idesa's emergence from bankruptcy protection.

At the least, the order bars Braskem from contributing the $71 million that the joint venture would receive when it exits bankruptcy protection.

It is unclear how much of the $283 million in DIP financing remains to be disbursed.

The ruling raises broader questions about the funds that Braskem already transferred to Braskem Idesa.

The question could become moot if Braskem can overturn the injunction or address the judge's concerns. Until then, Braskem Idesa could be left with a confirmed reorganization plan without the funds to consummate it and emerge from bankruptcy protection.

Braskem Idesa owns Ethylene XXI, an integrated polyethylene (PE) complex in Coatzacoalcos, Veracruz state, Mexico. It has an ethane cracker with a capacity of 1.05m tonnes/year of ethylene capacity plus downstream capacities of 750,000 tonnes/year of high-density polyethylene (HDPE) and 300,000 tonnes/year of low-density polyethylene (LDPE).

Braskem Idesa filed for bankruptcy protection in US Bankruptcy Court, Southern Texas District. The case number is 26-90762.

Braskem filed for out-of-court restructuring with the 2nd Bankruptcy and Judicial Reorganization Court of the Judicial District of the Capital of the State of Sao Paulo. In Brazil, the court is known as the 2ª Vara de Falencias e Recuperacoes Judiciais da Comarca da Capital do Estado de Sao Paulo.

The company's restructuring plan can be found here.

Braskem has also filed legal proceedings under Chapter 15 in US Bankruptcy Court, Southern District of New York. The number for the lead case is 26-11522.

Based in Brazil, Braskem makes polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP), polyvinyl chloride (PVC) and many commodity chemicals.
