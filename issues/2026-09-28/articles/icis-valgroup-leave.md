HEADLINE: Brazil's Valgroup 10-day collective leave at Lorena plant 'a one-off, not a sector trend' – executive

PUBLISHED: 25-Sep-2026

ATTRIBUTION: Jonathan Lopez (ICIS)

BODY:

SAO PAULO (ICIS)–Valgroup said a 10-day collective leave at its laminated film plant in Lorena, Sao Paulo state, is a one-off measure and does not signal a wider slowdown in the sector, a director at the Brazilian packaging producer said to ICIS.

In a written response, Eduardo Berkovitz, director for institutional relations and compliance at Valgroup, said the leave will run from 5 to 15 October and will affect about 65% of the plant's workforce.

"This is a one-off move specific to this unit. There was a slight reduction in the order backlog — normal for this time of year — which coincided with productivity gains achieved in recent months," said Berkovitz.

"These factors, combined with the need for preventive maintenance, prompted the scheduling of a collective vacation period. This measure should not be interpreted as an indicator of a broader industry trend."

Brazil's labor law allows companies – mostly in industrial sectors – to implement 'collective vacation' to manager their output.

Brazilian formally-employed workers are granted around 30 days/year of annual leave. Within the industrial sectors, as work is a 'collective' activity, vacation periods can be organized by the employer in groups of employees.

Operations in Lorena will resume once the 10-day period ends, and the time will be used for maintenance on equipment and the plant's infrastructure.

Berkovitz added no other Valgroup facility is implementing collective leave.

The executive did not respond to a question about the stoppage being potentially linked to lower demand and a smaller client portfolio, which the company mentioned in a document about the stoppage sent to the Chemical Workers' Union.

"These is just a collective leave scheduled for a 10-day period, with operations resuming at the end of that time. The decision addresses the specific conditions mentioned and applies to only a portion of the unit's workforce," he concluded.

Valgroup makes plastic packaging, including films, preforms, caps and bottles serving sectors such food, beverage, personal care and manufacturing.
