HEADLINE: Brazil court blocks Braskem funding for Mexican unit's US bankruptcy plan

PUBLISHED: 25-Sep-2026

ATTRIBUTION: Jonathan Lopez (ICIS)

BODY:

SAO PAULO (ICIS)–A Sao Paulo bankruptcy judge ordered Brazilian petrochemicals major Braskem to immediately halt payments and guarantees supporting the US Chapter 11 restructuring of its Mexican subsidiary, polyethylene (PE) producer Braskem Idesa.

- Court blocks Braskem's $800 million support package for Mexican unit
- Two Braskem units lose creditor protection after missing adhesion threshold
- Judge summons Petrobras and IG4 fund to explain deal approvals

Judge Taina Maria Leonardo de Oliveira granted an injunction requested by a group of Braskem noteholders and debenture holders known as the Ad Hoc Group.

The support package involves commitments worth about $800 million.

The decision came on the same day a US bankruptcy court in the Southern District of Texas was scheduled to hold a final hearing to confirm Braskem Idesa's plan, but it was published before that hearing took place.

Braskem Idesa is a joint venture between Braskem (75% stake) and Mexico's Grupo Idesa (with the remaining 25%).

The Sao Paulo judge barred Braskem SA, Braskem Netherlands BV and the other debtors in Braskem's out-of-court restructuring from carrying out what the court calls "Operation Bakide" until further notice.

The operation includes $415 million in debtor-in-possession financing, a $71 million exit equity contribution from Braskem, and put options granting Braskem Idesa's creditors the right to sell stakes in the Mexican unit five to six years after the reorganization.

The injunction prohibits new payments, transfers of funds or assets, debtor-in-possession (DIP) disbursements, the exit contribution, new guarantees and any commitments linked to put and call options on Braskem Idesa shares.

The court said violations could result in daily fines and personal liability for executives.

In her ruling, the judge said the commitments were being implemented during the stay period, when Braskem's creditors are barred from enforcing their claims, and said Braskem had not shown an equivalent economic return to its Brazilian companies' cash or given creditors prior transparency.

She added that final approval in the US could irreversibly drain liquidity, and that the order applied only to the Brazilian debtors, not to the foreign proceedings.

Public prosecutors had backed the request.

The judge also gave Braskem 15 days to file a detailed report on the operation's origin, consideration and impact. She summoned its controlling shareholders, state-owned oil major Petrobras and investment fund Shine I FIP (an arm of IG4 Capital), to explain within the same period the corporate and contractual approvals behind the deal.

In a separate part of the decision, the court partially upheld challenges from Banco Safra and holders of notes issued by Braskem America Finance Company, ruling that the restructuring cannot pool creditor support across the group's six companies, so creditor adhesion must be counted company by company.

As a result, the judge revoked the stay period for Braskem Netherlands BV and Braskem America Finance Company. Their creditor adhesion stood at 20.4% and 13.2% respectively, below the legal minimum of one-third. The two units have 15 days to show sufficient support or propose another course of action. Adhesion at Braskem SA stood at 39.6%.

Citing the case's "extraordinary complexity", the court appointed ACFB Administracao Judicial as judicial administrator, an unusual step in out-of-court restructurings.

Braskem reported total liabilities of Brazilian reais (R) 187.1 billion ($36 billion), including roughly R56.5 billion in financial claims subject to the plan and R130.6 billion in intercompany debt.

In a statement, Braskem said, "The company has not yet been formally served notice of the decision, which remains pending official publication. The company is assessing the consequences and next steps and will keep the market informed of any material developments."

Petrobras and IG4 had not responded to a request for comment at the time of writing.
