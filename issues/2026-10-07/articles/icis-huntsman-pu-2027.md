HEADLINE: INTERVIEW: US Huntsman expects flat PU demand for 2027

PUBLISHED: 06-Oct-2026

ATTRIBUTION: Al Greenwood (ICIS)

BODY:

ORLANDO (ICIS)–Huntsman expects demand for its polyurethane products to remain flat in 2027, a reflection of the unlikely prospects of a recovery from the automotive and building and construction sectors, an executive said on Tuesday.

The lack of affordable housing has depressed sales of new and existing homes. Without a decline in mortgage rates, it is difficult to see how housing can become more affordable for consumers and drive sales, said Doug Ellerbusch, vice president, Polyurethanes Americas, Huntsman.

"As long as there's an affordability issue, I don't think we're going to see any type of massive upswings in new construction builds," he said.

Ellerbusch made his comments in an interview with ICIS on the sidelines of the Polyurethanes Technical Conference, hosted by the Center for the Polyurethanes Industry (CPI).

New house construction drives demand for rigid polyurethane foam that is used as insulation as well as for oriented strand board (OSB).

Existing house sales drive demand for furniture and bedding, other large end markets for polyurethanes, according to ICIS.

Ellerbusch raised concerns about whether the lack of affordable housing could cause fundamental changes in consumer habits and expectations. The danger is that consumers stop aspiring for home ownership. If such a trend takes hold, then it could cause fundamental shifts in demand for housing.

The lack of affordable homes has created an opportunity in multi-family construction, said Hende Azene, marketing and commercial excellence director, Americas, Huntsman.

Over the past couple of years, polyurethanes demand has been growing for such projects, Ellerbusch said. More recently, demand growth has become lumpier.

DATA CENTERS BECOME GROWING SOURCE OF DEMAND

The construction of data centers has become a small but fast-growing source of new demand for polyurethane chemicals used to make insulated metal panels and polyisocyanurate board, Ellerbusch said.

Other bright spots include infrastructure projects, Azene said.

AUTOS SHIFT TOWARDS PREMIUM

Polyurethane demand from automobiles should remain steady, but the composition is changing.

Azene said producers are favoring premium models, and these tend to consume more polyurethanes per vehicle.

So far, Huntsman is seeing few signs that higher fuel prices are shifting buying habits toward electric vehicles (EVs), Ellerbusch said.

This likely reflects the uncertainty surrounding US policy, he said. "It feels a little bit like a standstill at this point until that gets sorted out."

US PO, CO DISRUPTIONS GET RESOLVED

Despite recent disruptions in upstream supplies of propylene oxide (PO) and carbon monoxide (CO), Huntsman has continued supplying customers with methylene diphenyl diisocyanate (MDI) without interruption, said Christy Blanchard, operations director, Americas, Huntsman. The company did not declare force majeure.

"We've been able to navigate any type of shortages whether it was CO or PO with no impact to our customers and definitely not having to declare force majeure," she said.

The disruptions have since been resolved.

That said, polyurethane producers in general are contending with higher feedstock costs.

Benzene is one of the main chemicals used to make MDI, and US material has been trading above $5/gal, according to ICIS.

GROWING IN A CHALLENGING MARKET

Elevated benzene costs are among the latest challenges that the polyurethanes industry has contended with.

The housing market is in a multi-year.

ICIS data shows that excess supplies of polyurethane chemicals will persist at least through the end of the decade.

In 2025, the US adopted its Liberation Day tariffs on imports from nearly every country in the world.

The new duties were especially disruptive to Ellerbusch downstream customers, Ellerbusch said.

The war in Iran has proven to be more disruptive to MDI producers because of the subsequent constraints on shipments of crude oil and fuel. These, in turn, led to US benzene contract prices reaching highs last seen in 2022.

In such a market, companies are seeking ways to distinguish themselves.

Ellerbusch highlighted Huntsman's MDI operations in Geismar, Louisiana state, US, which continued to supply customers with material despite upstream outages.

"That supply security piece is something we take very seriously," he said.

Another path to growth is product substitution. Huntsman has already seen success with replacing formaldehyde-based binders with those made with MDI for OSB.

The Polyurethanes Technical Conference ends on Wednesday.
