# HEADLINE
INSIGHT: Thai plastics recyclers expand across value chain as domestic usage grows

# PUBLISHED
30-Sep-2026

# ATTRIBUTION
Jonathan Yee

# BODY

SINGAPORE (ICIS)–Thailand's plastic recycling companies are seeking to further incorporate post-consumer (PCR) content into their products through sourcing of suitable waste feedstock within the country and enhancing production of recyclates.

- Indovida plans capacity for about 400 million rPET preforms/year
- HaadThip orders more than 1,000 tonnes of recycled PET pellets, about 10 times above pilot project volume
- ThaiPlastic, Union J Plus secure FDA "no-objection" letter

Several firms including global polyethylene terephthalate (PET) major Indorama are advancing plastics recycling at several points in the value chain, from streamlining production of recyclates to developing new packaging technology and processes.

**DIRECT ROUTE FROM FLAKES TO PREFORMS**

Indovida (Thailand), a joint venture established by Thai recycling producer Indorama Ventures and Japanese industrial company Iwatani in July 2026, plans to use flake-to-preform (FtoP) direct recycling technology developed by Japanese beverage manufacturer Suntory and its partners.

The process makes PET preforms from recycled flakes, mainly to be sourced through Indorama, which are then formed into bottles.

Indovida (Thailand) expects annual capacity of about 400 million recycled polyethylene terephthalate (rPET) preforms, using flakes mainly sourced through Indorama Ventures in 2028. Suntory PepsiCo Beverage (Thailand), a joint venture between US and Japanese beverage giants, is to procure the preforms for products sold in Thailand.

The partners describe the project as the first commercial deployment of FtoP technology for PET bottles in southeast Asia.

The technology eliminates certain crystallization and drying steps used in conventional PET recycling.

Suntory estimates that bottles made using the process produce about 70% less carbon dioxide than bottles made from virgin fossil-based material, based on Japan operations. Suntory had adjusted bottle-selection and decontamination processes to handle the varying quality of bottles collected in Thailand, it added.

**STREAMLINED SOURCING**

HaadThip, which manufactures and distributes Coca-Cola beverages across 14 provinces in southern Thailand, signed an agreement on 16 September with Oxytech, a collector and processor of post-consumer materials, and Royce Universal, a recycled polyethylene terephthalate (rPET) pellet producer.

The agreement moves the three companies' "Southern Recycling Alliance" from a pilot to an ongoing sourcing arrangement.

In the pilot project launched in September 2025, used PET bottles weighing a total of 100 tonnes were collected in southern Thailand which were then recycled into rPET resins for new beverage packaging.

Under the new arrangement, HaadThip can order rPET pellets directly from Royce Universal, which coordinates with Oxytech to source baled bottles or PET flakes.

Oxytech supplies the material for conversion into pellets at Royce Universal's facility in Nakhon Pathom province, followed by HaadThip transporting the pellets to its Punpin plant in Surat Thani for packaging production.

In the pilot project, Oxytech sourced and processed the bottles into flakes, which Royce Universal converted into food-grade rPET pellets. HaadThip used the pellets to make bottles at its Punpin plant in Surat Thani. This was completed in the second quarter of 2026.

HaadThip said it had already ordered more than 1,000 tonnes of rPET pellets during discussions on expanding the alliance. The volume was more than 10 times the volume of the initial project.

According to market sources, the output for a single production line in an rPET plant ranges from 1,000-1,200 tonnes of rPET pellets per month.

"During the second quarter of this year, the PET market experienced volatility resulting from the situation in the Middle East. This drove up virgin PET prices and raised concerns about supply continuity, reinforcing the importance of having diverse and reliable sourcing options," HaadThip CEO Patchara Rattakul said.

"rPET is an important alternative that helps us respond more effectively to market volatility," Rattakul added.

HaadThip said it aims to use 35-40% recycled content in its primary packaging and support collection of bottles and cans equivalent to 70-75% of the volume it places on the market by 2035.

The partners also said stronger collection would help prepare for a possible Thai extended producer responsibility (EPR) framework, which is yet to be launched.

"The implementation of mandates and increased awareness of [EPR] in various Asian countries have been key drivers in the growth of recycling, Thailand included," ICIS principal analyst Salmon Aidan Lee said.

"This momentum is expected to gain pace in coming years, and more adoption of recycled use and investments are expected, as reflected in the ICIS Recycling Supply Tracker," Lee added.

**FOOD-CONTACT CERTIFICATIONS FOR FLAKES**

Further developments have emerged regarding food-contact certifications for flakes produced by a growing number of Thai recycling companies. These could expand their access to customers in southeast Asia and North America, according to market sources.

Thai polyolefins recycler Union J Plus announced in August that its mechanical recycling process for post-consumer high-density polyethylene (HDPE) and polypropylene (PP) has received a "no-objection letter" (NOL) from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA).

While NOL will not have an impact on overall operating rates and production processes outside of what has been validated, this certification will open up commercial opportunities for these Thai recyclers to market/sell products to customers which have stringent food-contact product needs, like in the US.

The FDA's opinion covers specified uses and conditions, including contact with certain dry foods, rather than unrestricted food-contact use.

Union J Plus currently operates with a processing capacity of 5,000 tonnes/year of food-grade recycled HDPE and PP; in addition to its 7,200 tonnes/year of high quality post-consumer recycled (PCR) low-density polyethylene (LDPE) and linear low-density polyethylene (LLDPE).

In the same month, ThaiPlastic Recycle Group also received an FDA no-objection letter in August for its rPET flakes production process, subject to specified feedstock and end-use conditions.

According to ICIS data, the total nameplate capacity for its rPET flake production stands at 36,000 tonnes/year.

**RECYCLING TECHNOLOGIES GAIN MOMENTUM**

Other Thai producers are also pursuing recycling technologies to support more circular materials management.

In June 2026, the Thai subsidiary of US plastics recycling company PureCycle Technologies, appointed engineering firm Wood for front-end engineering and design (FEED) work on a planned polypropylene (PP) dissolution-recycling facility.

PureCycle (Thailand) is developing the facility with Thai petrochemical producer IRPC at the latter's eco-industrial site, and Wood's work includes designing its integration with the existing petrochemical complex.

Wood said the proposed line could produce up to 130 million pounds/year of recycled polypropylene (rPP).

At the time of the announcement, construction was expected to begin in the second half of 2026, with ramping up of operations expected in 2028.

According to PureCycle, its dissolution recycling technology "removes color, odor, additives and other materials from post-consumer polypropylene to create a recycled resin with properties comparable to new plastic".
