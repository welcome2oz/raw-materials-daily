**HEADLINE:** Russia may ease diesel export ban if overproduction occurs

**PUBLISHED:** Oct 02, 2026, 06:33 AM

**ATTRIBUTION:** Editor Vlad Schepkov, Investing.com

**BODY:**

Russia said Friday it may partially lift diesel export restrictions if production exceeds domestic demand, offering potential relief to strained global fuel markets.

Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak told the upper chamber of the Russian parliament that the domestic diesel market is currently balanced. Russia extended its ban on diesel exports for fuel producers through the end of October earlier this week.

The country was the world's second-largest diesel exporter after the United States before it began limiting overseas sales this summer to meet domestic market demand. Ukrainian attacks on refineries reduced supplies available for the domestic market.

"Today, the diesel market is balanced and supplies are sufficient. Although we extended the diesel export ban into October just a few days ago, we will continue to monitor the situation and, if diesel production exceeds domestic demand, we will consider partially reopening exports," Novak said.

Global fuel shortages and sharp price increases have affected markets, particularly in the United States, where diesel prices have reached records above $6.50 per gallon. The wars in Iran and Ukraine have constrained fuel deliveries, creating political risk for US President Donald Trump ahead of the midterm elections.

President Vladimir Putin said Thursday that Russia will not supply diesel to global energy markets until sanctions against Moscow are lifted.

Novak said the situation with Ukrainian drone attacks on Russia's energy infrastructure remains "tense." Russia prevented attacks on four refineries overnight, while authorities were assessing damage at one plant.

Damage from repeated raids on refineries has been lower due to protection measures, Novak said. "This allows us to restore damaged equipment and bring facilities back into operation more quickly after repairs," he said.
