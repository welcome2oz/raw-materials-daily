**HEADLINE:** G7 nations agree 100 million barrel crude and diesel release

**PUBLISHED:** 02-Oct-2026

**ATTRIBUTION:** Tom Brown

**BODY:**

LONDON (ICIS)–The G7 countries agreed on Friday to release 100 million barrels of crude oil and diesel fuel over the next four months from their strategic reserves in a bid to stabilise pricing and balance supplies in the short term.

The precise split between crude and diesel was not disclosed, but G7 members and partners will make a "substantial" diesel release in the first 20 days.

In a statement, the group committed to allowing energy products to continue to flow between G7 countries, following the US government's move to consider an export ban on diesel in a bid to bring down domestic prices from historic highs.

"We reaffirm our commitment to refrain from export restrictions on energy and energy products between G7 countries and call on all producers to refrain from imposing bans that could exacerbate market tensions," the G7 said.

Despite supply shock fears for oil products in the wake of ongoing conflict on the Strait of Hormuz and falling traffic through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait following hostilities between Houthi forces and Saudi Arabia, sanctions on Russia are expected to be maintained.

The 100 million barrel pledge is "taking into account commitments that have already been fulfilled", the statement said, referring to the March 2026 International Energy Administration (IEA) member country commitments to release 400 million barrels of oil from strategic reserves.

The partner countries will also co-ordinate refinery maintenance schedules across the G7 "and temporarily increase utilization rates where feasible", the statement added.

The G7 comprises Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the UK and the US.
