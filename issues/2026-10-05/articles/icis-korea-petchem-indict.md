HEADLINE: South Korea indicts six petrochemical firms over W16.4 trillion price-fixing case – report

PUBLISHED: 02-Oct-2026

ATTRIBUTION: Jonathan Yee, ICIS

BODY:

SINGAPORE (ICIS)–South Korean prosecutors have indicted six petrochemical companies and 37 of their current and former executives and employees for allegedly fixing prices of products, including polyvinyl chloride (PVC) and plasticizers.

The companies charged are LG Chem, PKC, OCI, Aekyung Chemical, Lotte Fine Chemical and Unid, Yonhap News Agency reported. The Seoul Central District Prosecutors' Office announced the charges on 1 October.

Prosecutors allege that the companies colluded on prices of eight petrochemical products, worth about won (W) 16.4 trillion ($12.2 billion), from January 2021 to April 2026, Yonhap said.

It is the largest cartel case uncovered in the country, according to the report.

LG Chem declined to comment. The other five indicted companies did not respond to an ICIS request for comment.

Besides PVC and plasticizers, the products include caustic soda and hydrochloric acid, Yonhap said.

The companies allegedly agreed on how much and when to change supply prices, and allegedly fixed bids for tenders held by large customers, according to prosecutors.

The talks were mainly held among team leader-level staff, who agreed on quoted prices and even set the order in which each company would raise prices, Yonhap said.

Prosecutors also found that one executive ordered staff to delete words related to the collusion from messaging chat rooms after the investigation began, Yonhap noted.

Two former senior executives – a former head of PKC's sales division and a former head of LG Chem's business division – were already under arrest when they were indicted, Yonhap said. Those indicted without detention include OCI vice chairman and CEO Kim Yu-shin and former PKC CEO Jang Young-soo.

Hanwha Solutions was also among the companies under investigation but was not indicted, as it was said to have benefited from a leniency programme for companies that self-report, according to Yonhap.

Prosecutors said the collusion continued even after some of the companies received about W146.5 billion in government support amid the US-Iran war pushing up international oil product prices.

"Even though the US-Iran war caused a crisis for the national economy, they saw it only as a chance to improve their profits," said Na Hee-seok, chief prosecutor of the office's Fair Trade Investigation Department, during a press conference on 1 October.

Indicted cases by the office will be tried at the Seoul Central District Court. A trial date has not been announced.

Prosecutors began the investigation with raids on the seven companies in early August.
