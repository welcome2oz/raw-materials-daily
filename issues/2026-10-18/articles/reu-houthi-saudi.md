HEADLINE: Yemen’s Houthis hit palace in Aden, coalition details attacks on Saudi infrastructure
PUBLISHED: Published Oct 15, 2026, 04:06 PM
ATTRIBUTION: By Enas Alashray, Mohammed Ghobari and Eman Abouhassira, CAIRO/DUBAI, Oct 15 (Reuters) (Investing.com 전재)
BODY:
CAIRO/DUBAI, Oct 15 (Reuters) - Yemen’s Houthis fired missiles at the presidential palace complex in the southern city of Aden on Thursday, killing five people, the government said, as the Saudi-led coalition listed dozens of Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure in recent weeks.

The attack was the first in the current conflict between the Iran-aligned Houthis and Saudi Arabia on Aden’s Al-Maashiq palace, the seat of Yemen’s Saudi-backed and internationally recognised government.

The conflict is a new theatre in the wider regional war that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in February.

Yemen’s prime minister and cabinet members were safe following the attack, Yemeni Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani said.

Witnesses told Reuters they heard loud booms near the palace.

Yemen’s cabinet said the attack involved missiles and drones.

Yemen’s health minister Qasim Buhaibeh said five people were killed and 16 wounded in what he described as a Houthi attack on the government’s official headquarters at the presidential complex.

The casualties included guards and civilian employees.

Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said its fighters had bombed what he described as the Saudi military operations headquarters in Aden, as part of the group’s declared policy of targeting "Saudi reinforcements wherever they were located".

The Houthi-run news agency SABA said the headquarters was run by Saudi officers and that there were weapons depots there.

Reuters could not independently verify the claims.

Fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed forces resumed last month, and aid agencies have warned of rapidly worsening conditions in Yemen.
