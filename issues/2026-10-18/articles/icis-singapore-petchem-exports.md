HEADLINE: Singapore Sept petrochemical exports fall anew; feedstock risks weigh on outlook
PUBLISHED: 16-Oct-2026
ATTRIBUTION: Nurluqman Suratman (ICIS)
BODY:
SINGAPORE (ICIS)–Singapore’s petrochemical exports in September declined by 8.3% year on year to Singapore dollar (S$) 808.7 million ($632 million), with future production facing risks amid prolonged shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz.

September marked a 19th consecutive month of year-on-year declines in petrochemical exports, with annual growth last recorded in February 2025, Enterprise Singapore data showed.

The decline in petrochemical shipments last month, however, eased from a 21.9% contraction recorded in August.

The downturn in the southeast Asian country’s petrochemical sector is expected to persist as the continuing US-Iran conflict adds to feedstock uncertainty and weak margins, according to research firm BMI, a unit of Fitch Solutions.

“We now expect a preliminary resolution [to the US-Iran war] in Q1 2027, from Q3 2026 previously.

This will delay the recovery in Singapore’s petrochemicals sector, which remains constrained by feedstock uncertainty and weak margins,” BMI said.

Renewed disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz could further constrain feedstock supplies to Singapore’s petrochemical sector and delay its recovery, it added.

Singapore is a leading petrochemical manufacturing and trading hub in southeast Asia, with more than 100 international chemical companies operating on Jurong Island, including ExxonMobil and Aster Chemicals & Energy.

Chemicals was one of only two manufacturing clusters, alongside biomedical manufacturing, to contract during the third quarter, according to the Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry (METI) on 14 October.

Overall Q3 manufacturing growth slowed to 7.5% year on year from 12.9% in Q2, METI said.

Singapore posted a 5.4% annualized Q3 GDP growth, slowing from a 6.2% expansion in Q2, advance estimates from METI showed.

“Growth during the quarter was supported by output expansions in the electronics, precision engineering, transport engineering and general manufacturing clusters, which outweighed output declines in the chemicals and biomedical manufacturing clusters,” METI said.
