HEADLINE: Spain's Tarragona chemicals hub mayors demand chemical risk mitigation strategy overhaul
PUBLISHED: 16-Oct-2026
ATTRIBUTION: Jonathan Lopez (ICIS)
BODY:
SAO PAULO (ICIS)–Mayors from towns around Spain's Tarragona petrochemicals hub have called on the Catalan government to rethink how it manages risk from chemical plants and the transport of dangerous goods.

In a joint manifesto, the mayors warned that recent changes to emergency planning could hit homes, public facilities and local development.

In a written response to ICIS, the regional government in Spain's northeast region said the recent revision of PLASEQTA, the external emergency plan for the chemical sector in Camp de Tarragona region, had barely changed the fundamental elements of the previous plan.

The AEQT trade group for chemicals companies in the Camp de Tarragona had not responded to a request for comment at the time of writing.

In a manifesto signed at a meeting in Vila-seca, the leaders of eight municipalities, including Tarragona, Salou, Constanti and La Canonja, said safety policy should focus on cutting risk at its source rather than only setting distances and restrictions on surrounding land.

Safety policy cannot consist solely of determining what cannot be done in a potentially affected area.

The prior question must be what we can do about the source of the risk, the infrastructure or the operation so that the risk decreases," the mayors wrote.

This is not about pitting safety against development.

It is about using knowledge, technology, investment and institutional co-ordination to make them compatible.

We municipalities accept the responsibilities that fall to us and demand the same level of commitment from the Generalitat de Catalunya [regional authorities] and all actors involved in the generation, prevention and management of risks.

The signatories said PLASEQTA's revision had shown that "certain decisions on risk identification and management" can have direct consequences for established spaces, facilities and activities in their municipalities.

Protecting people must always be the top priority.
