HEADLINE: Global diesel shortages could persist well into 2027, IEA says
PUBLISHED: Published Oct 14, 2026, 08:02 AM
ATTRIBUTION: By Robert Harvey, LONDON, Oct 14 (Reuters) (Investing.com 전재)
BODY:
LONDON, Oct 14 (Reuters) - Global diesel markets could remain tight well into next year, with resulting high prices driving inflation and hurting economic growth, as conflicts curb output from the Middle East and Russia, the International Energy Agency said on Wednesday.

The agency’s warning comes on the eve of a meeting that will discuss the scale and timing of releases from the agency’s emergency stocks aimed at calming energy markets.

Diesel is used widely in industry, agriculture, and motoring, meaning its record-high prices have created both political and economic pressure.

US President Donald Trump’s administration has told European Union countries to draw down inventories or face a potential US diesel export ban.

Record diesel prices are already having broad economic consequences by raising the cost of goods and impacting real incomes, economic activity, and inflation, the agency, which advises industrialised countries and manages their emergency oil stocks, said in a monthly report.

Combined losses of diesel exports from the Middle East and Russia totalled 1.3 million barrels per day in September, the agency said.

That represents about 4.5% of the global diesel and gas oil demand the agency forecasts for this year.

The diesel market remained under significant strain as the prolonged supply disruptions have caused inventories to be drawn down at a time of year when they usually rise, the IEA said.

As the remaining buffers in the system continue to erode, in the absence of a resolution to the conflicts in the Middle East, oil markets may experience more turmoil in the months ahead before any eventual surplus emerges in 2027," the IEA said.

A recovery in global refinery operations expected towards the end of this year should start to replenish stocks, the IEA said, although this will be insufficient to offset the deficit accumulated in middle distillates markets.

Diesel, gasoil and jet fuel all fall under the middle distillates category.

The global gasoil supply and demand balance will remain tight in the first half of 2027, and ease in the second half, the IEA report showed.
