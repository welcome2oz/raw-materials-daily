HEADLINE: Asia petrochemical demand likely tepid after China Oct holiday

PUBLISHED: 29-Sep-2026

ATTRIBUTION: Jonathan Yee, ICIS

BODY:

SINGAPORE (ICIS)–Asia's petrochemical demand is expected to remain sluggish after China's week-long holiday in October, as elevated and volatile feedstock and freight costs continue to squeeze downstream margins.

- Naphtha demand buoyed by some margin improvement
- For most products, China inventories may build up post-holiday
- China property downturn weighs on PVC, broader consumer demand

Restricted availability due to reduced regional production amid the US-Iran war in the Middle East is supporting some markets this week.

But cautious restocking and weak end-use demand point to limited post-holiday buying.

Across markets, supply could lengthen as some plants restart and new capacity comes on stream in the fourth quarter, unless consumption improves.

**NAPHTHA DEMAND FIRM**

As regional naphtha supplies remain constrained due to ongoing tensions between the US and Iran in the Middle East, Asian cracker operators are diversifying their import sources, which now include Europe, Africa, India and the Americas, as demand for the petrochemical feedstock has firmed.

A recovery in the ethylene-naphtha spread has provided some support to cracker economics and, consequently, to feedstock naphtha demand.

The spread returned to a healthy range on 18 September after remaining weak earlier in the month and has stayed positive since, according to ICIS data.

Naphtha prices will nevertheless remain vulnerable to crude oil volatility driven by geopolitical developments.

**WEAK DEMAND, RISING SUPPLY CLOUD POST-HOLIDAY OUTLOOK**

Firm naphtha demand was in stark contrast with downstream conditions – squeezed margins due to high costs and weak end-use consumption constrained production.

Weak sentiment prevailed in China's polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP), 2-ethylhexanol (2-EH), acetone and other markets ahead of the October holiday, with buyers largely purchasing on a needs-only basis despite the traditionally stronger September-October demand season.

China will be on holiday from 1-7 October for National Day. Migrant workers in China typically head back to their hometowns during this time as most manufacturing plants shut down.

If domestic supply lengthens, Chinese producers may seek to place more cargoes in southeast Asia, south Asia and other northeast Asian markets, although export flows will depend on freight costs, port conditions and prices.

Some southeast Asian converters appear to prefer local cargoes in their procurement to ensure timely delivery.

Escalating Middle East tensions recently pushed Chinese styrene domestic and export prices sharply higher, but loss-making downstream producers remain reluctant to accept higher feedstock costs.

Operating rates at Chinese expandable polystyrene (EPS), polystyrene (PS) and acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) plants remain low at around 50-57% since July, limiting styrene consumption.

Attention will turn to producer inventories when trading resumes in Chinese markets on 8 October.

Given expectations of some plants restarting from turnarounds, accompanied by longer shutdown of several downstream plants, product inventories in China could build up and force suppliers to move cargoes at lower prices if domestic demand remain limited.

Sluggish consumption, weak investment and a worsening property sector offset gains from AI-driven manufacturing and exports in the world's second-biggest economy.

Retail sales growth slowed to 0.4% year on year in August from 0.6% in July, marking a second consecutive month of deceleration and leaving year-to-date growth at 1.1%.

China's ongoing property sectors slump is weighing on demand for polyvinyl chloride (PVC), and a post-holiday rebound is seen as unlikely, according to market sources.
