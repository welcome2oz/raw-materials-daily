HEADLINE: Country Snapshots - Advancing Kigali Amendment Goals: Bhutan and Ecuador

PUBLISHED: 05 Oct 2026

ATTRIBUTION: UN Environment

BODY:

As the world marks the 10th anniversary of the Kigali Amendment, these ten country experiences showcase how countries are turning global commitments into tangible climate action. From strengthening policies and customs controls to training technicians, promoting refrigerant recovery and accelerating the adoption of climate-friendly cooling technologies, each experience reflects innovation, collaboration and determination in addressing the challenge of high-global-warming-potential refrigerants. Together, these accounts demonstrate that the Kigali Amendment is more than an environmental agreement; it is a catalyst for sustainable cooling, green skills, stronger institutions and climate resilience. As we celebrate a decade of progress, these country cases highlight the people, partnerships and practical solutions that are helping to build a cooler, more sustainable future for all.

Beginning this week and leading up to the 38th Meeting of the Parties, OzonAction will feature a special weekly series on its website, "10 Years, 10 Perspectives," highlighting the experiences, achievements, and lessons learned by 10 countries in implementing the Kigali Amendment.

This week, we turn the spotlight on Bhutan and Ecuador to explore how they are advancing their efforts to achieve the goals of the Kigali Amendment.

BHUTAN: BUILDING A FUTURE-READY RAC SERVICING WORKFORCE

Bhutan is strengthening its Refrigeration and Air-Conditioning (RAC) servicing workforce to support the safe transition towards climate-friendly cooling. Working with technical training institutes and the national technical and vocational education and training authority, the National Ozone Unit has supported training on good servicing practices, refrigerant recovery, leak detection and prevention, energy efficiency, and the safe handling of low- Global Warming Potential (GWP) and flammable refrigerants. Training centres have also been assessed and provided with tools and equipment to support competency-based education.

In 2024, 60 RAC servicing technicians from government, civil society and the private sector were trained, while six trainers from technical training institutes received competency-based assessment training. In early 2026, an additional 15 technicians participated in a training-of-trainers workshop on refrigerant recovery, leak detection, and energy efficiency. Furthermore, in September 2026, 10 trainers and assessors participated in a two-day training on competency-based training and assessment, including an introduction to energy-auditing practices, while an additional 11 technicians participated in the two-day training on good servicing practices and energy-efficiency in the RAC sector. Altogether, 21 trainers, assessors and technicians were trained through this four-day training workshop.

This is a practical example on the ground of how by strengthening both technicians and the institutions that train them, Bhutan is building national capacity to reduce refrigerant emissions and safely service emerging cooling technologies.

ECUADOR: LINKING TRAINTER DEVELOPMENT WITH REFRIGERANT REGENERATION

Ecuador has combined training-of-trainers programmes with refrigerant recovery, regeneration and the safe handling of lower-GWP alternatives. The Ministry of Economic and Productive Development, through its National Ozone Unit, the United Nations Industrial Development Organization (UNIDO), universities and technical institutions, have helped develop national training capacity, with dozens of instructors trained and nearly one tonne of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) reportedly regenerated.

The initiative links workforce development with improved refrigerant management, circular-economy practices and the transition towards climate-friendly cooling.
