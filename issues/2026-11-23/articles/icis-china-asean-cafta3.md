HEADLINE: China-ASEAN upgraded trace pact takes effect 1 December
PUBLISHED: 20-Nov-2026
ATTRIBUTION: Jonathan Yee, SINGAPORE (ICIS)
BODY (verbatim pieces, in page order):
SINGAPORE (ICIS)–The China-ASEAN Free Trade Area (CAFTA) 3.0 Upgrade Protocol will enter into force on 1 December 2026
giving the two sides a broader set of trade rules as US tariffs and protectionist measures reshape global trade.

The date of implementation was announced at the 29th ASEAN-China Summit in Manila on 16 November.

The protocol was signed in Kuala Lumpur on 28 October 2025,
after negotiations launched in November 2022 and concluded in May 2025.

The CAFTA 3.0 upgrade extends the free trade area beyond tariff cuts into areas including the digital economy,
green economy, supply chain connectivity, standards and conformity assessment,
customs procedures and trade facilitation, competition and consumer protection,
small and medium-sized enterprises and economic and technical cooperation.

Among key provisions in the agreement are digital trade rules covering single windows, e-invoicing, e-payments
and artificial intelligence (AI), and a green economy framework with eight priority areas
including new energy vehicles (EVs) and photovoltaics.

The protocol involves no direct market access concessions, instead marking a shift from “market opening”
to “institutional building”, with deeper alignment of rules and standards,
according to an official at China’s Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM).

The upgrade takes effect as US President Donald Trump’s administration pushes for heavy tariffs on trade partners,
pushing countries to seek alternative trade links.

Chinese Premier Li Qiang said at the Manila summit that China and ASEAN would stay committed to multilateralism
and free trade even as “economic and trade restrictions have substantially increased around the world”.

Two-way trade exceeded $1 trillion in 2025, up 20.1% from 2021, and grew 29.6% in the first 10 months of 2026,
while cumulative two-way investment has topped $500 billion, the MOFCOM official said.

China, the world’s second-biggest economy, has been ASEAN’s largest trading partner for 17 consecutive years,
while ASEAN has been China’s biggest trading partner for six years, according to MOFCOM.
